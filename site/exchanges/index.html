The credential

What you are reading, and what you are not.

Definition

A LEXI credential is a co-signed legal opinion committed on-chain. It records who signed, for which jurisdiction, as of which date, until when, under which governing law and for whom, and it binds the opinion's scope and text with two hashes. It is not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator.

LEXI never sits in the listing decision; it tells you what the opinion covers, who signed it and whether it is still live. Compliance officers and listing teams read credentials; they never sign them. The opinion belongs to the licensed lawyer who co-signed it, its body stays off-chain and encrypted, and reading the public on-chain metadata is free.

Some listing teams require an opinion in writing; most run an internal legal review and keep a file. A PDF in that file cannot tell you whether the opinion is still current, whether the documents changed after signing, or whether your entity may rely on it. The credential records those three facts as a status, two hash commitments and a named-relier list. What listing pages publish is in the briefing how exchanges verify a token legal opinion.

  • Not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator. LEXI is not licensed or endorsed by VARA.
  • Not a listing signal. Whether an asset should be listed stays your decision.
  • Not a compliance score. Nothing on-chain rates the asset; the record holds the opinion's hashes and its status.
  • Not transferable. The token standard is undecided, so none is named here.

The three-step check

Read a credential in three steps.

One call against the credential record. Each step reads one on-chain field; the glossary defines the check in full.

  1. Status is Live, not Expired or Revoked.

    As-of date, expiry and revocation are on-chain.Does it still speak as of a date I accept?

  2. The scope and opinion hashes match the documents you hold.

    Hash the PDF and the scope text and compare them with the on-chain commitments.Is this the document the lawyer signed?

  3. Your entity is a named relier and has accepted the reliance terms.

    The credential names who may rely on it; the reliance record shows you accepted the terms.Am I expressly authorised to rely?

Live means unexpired and unrevoked, not that the opinion is a current legal conclusion; the as-of date and the regulator events after it tell you that. The specimen and the verify page, as designed, are on the credential page.

A worked relier

The Licensed Distributor, under VARA's Broker-Dealer Services Rulebook.

A Licensed Distributor is a VASP that VARA has licensed to carry out Broker-Dealer Services. Part IV of the Broker-Dealer Services Rulebook makes it satisfy itself about an asset before distributing it, keep its due diligence current, and redo and resubmit that diligence on a material change, through a fifteen Working Day Submission Period that is never a VARA approval. VARA asks for no legal opinion in that package; it asks for a third-party smart-contract audit.

What VARA does ask for is a file that stays current, and a dated opinion with a status fits that file: the as-of date says when the opinion spoke, the expiry and status say whether it still stands, and a material change means a re-issue, never an edit. A LEXI credential does not satisfy any VARA rule and is not a VARA approval. It is one dated, checkable item in a file that has to stay current.

Rule IV.B.1
Satisfy itself "beyond all reasonable doubt" before distributing a virtual asset.
Rule IV.D.2
A fifteen Working Day Submission Period for the distributor's submission to VARA.
Rule IV.D.7
Nothing in the submission process is a VARA approval of the asset.
Rule IV.E.1
Due diligence kept "up-to-date and accurate at all times".
Rule IV.E.3
Redo and resubmit the due diligence on a material change.
Part VIII, Market Conduct
Every VARA-licensed VASP publishes its own virtual-asset standards, keeps diligence records for eight years, and tells VARA when an asset no longer meets them.
