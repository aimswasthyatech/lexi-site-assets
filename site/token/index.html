Where revenue goes

Protocol USDC revenue, as designed.

Every service on LEXI is priced in USDC: protocols pay a USDC fee per credential, and reliers pay a fixed USDC fee for reliance access. Neither a protocol nor a relier ever needs to hold $LEXI, so a compliance bill is never denominated in the token. The protocol's USDC revenue then follows a fixed split. The split is a protocol parameter, and changing it is a governance matter.

What the token is for

Two roles: staking and governance.

Staking

A licensed lawyer posts an eligibility stake, a USD-pegged amount of about $5,000 in $LEXI, to be eligible to co-sign. The requirement is re-pegged so the dollar cost of participation stays stable, and unbonding takes 14 days. The stake is the bond that committee-adjudicated slashing acts on: proven misconduct only, capped in basis points. Reliers flag disputes; they do not slash. Only co-signers stake. What a licensed lawyer signs and stakes: for legal professionals.

Governance

Holders govern jurisdiction expansion, fee parameters, slashing conditions and treasury actions. Those four matters are the whole scope. The slashing conditions and the revenue split above are parameters that governance can change.

What holders never decide

Whether a protocol is compliant. That judgment belongs to the licensed lawyer who co-signs the opinion, and no vote changes it. No AI signs anything, and no holder vote stands in for a signature. Nothing about the token is needed to obtain, read or rely on a credential.

Why a token at all

Two reasons, stated flatly.

First, an open eligibility rule. Any licensed lawyer who passes the licensure check can post the stake and become eligible to co-sign. The rule is the same for every firm.

Second, the stake gives the co-signature economic weight that a directory listing does not have: a bond that misconduct can forfeit, under a committee's judgment, not an algorithm's.

Neither reason needs a customer to hold the token. Protocols pay in USDC, reliers pay in USDC, and reading public on-chain metadata is free.
