What you sign

A legal opinion, in your name, on a question you scoped.

The LEXI engine's first pass reaches you as input material: a checklist, a document review, a risk read. It is work product, labelled that way, and it is never the opinion. No opinion exists until you sign it, and no AI signs anything.

What exactly you are signing

A co-signed legal opinion: your conclusion, after your own diligence, on the question your scope defines. You review the engine first pass, test it, and co-sign only what your own judgment supports. The engine is in development on a proprietary corpus of real regulatory work; its output is prepared for you, not for the client.

Who your client is

Your firm's client is the protocol, exactly as it would be without LEXI. Collaboration runs on an engagement: a workspace tied to one protocol, one firm and one jurisdiction. Your firm assigns the signatory, the licensed lawyer authorised to co-sign. The protocol retains your firm directly and pays the retainer outside LEXI. LEXI takes no share of your fee, and 0% of protocol revenue goes to the co-signing lawyer.

What your exposure looks like

Tighter than an opinion letter you would write today, by design. Scope is hashed: the credential binds the precise question covered and what is excluded, so nobody can quietly stretch what you opined on. Reliers are named: reliance attaches only after a named relier accepts the reliance terms, and every reliance is recorded. By default you whitelist who may rely (Selective mode); Open reliance, if you ever choose it, requires a defined liability cap in the terms before it is offered. It is dated and it expires: your opinion speaks as of its date, and its on-chain status (Live, Expired, Revoked) is public, so no one relies on a stale opinion by accident.

The stake

A bond behind the signature, and narrow rules on what can touch it.

To be eligible to co-sign, a licensed lawyer posts an eligibility stake: a USD-pegged amount of about $5,000 in $LEXI, re-pegged so the dollar cost stays stable, with a 14-day unbonding period. It is a good-faith bond in the protocol design. No $LEXI token has been issued, so no stake is posted today.

The rules are deliberately narrow. Slashing is committee-adjudicated for proven misconduct only, capped in basis points. Reliers flag disputes; they do not decide them, and they cannot slash. Nobody is slashed because a regulator disagrees with an opinion. The slashing conditions are a governance matter.

Customers never need $LEXI; only co-signers stake. Stake documentation, without price, value or return talk: how $LEXI works.

What you get

Your hours go into judgment, not collation.

Work that arrives prepared

A protocol submits once. Its documentation is already structured and the engine's first pass is already run when the matter reaches you, on a corridor you already serve. The questions left are the ones only a licensed lawyer can answer.

A portable record of practice

Every credential you co-sign is a dated, public artifact: the signatory and firm ID, the jurisdiction, the as-of date and the status are on-chain. That record follows you, not a directory listing. The opinion body itself stays off-chain, encrypted, open only to named reliers.

A voice in governance

Staked lawyers take part in governing jurisdiction expansion, fee parameters, slashing conditions and the treasury. Holders never vote on whether a protocol is compliant; that question is yours alone, in the opinion you sign.

The open items

Two questions deserve a direct conversation, not marketing copy.

Professional indemnity cover, and your bar's view. How co-signing interacts with your professional indemnity insurance, and how your bar or licensing body treats participation, differ by jurisdiction. We would rather work through your specifics than publish a blanket assurance. Raise them in your application and we will address them before you commit anything.

For context, not endorsement: VARA's Regulations 2023 (Part IV.A.5) treat duly registered practising lawyers as professionals authorised by a competent professional body who maintain professional indemnity insurance, which is the same bar LEXI's licensure check and the open insurance item describe. LEXI is not licensed or endorsed by VARA.

Questions licensed lawyers ask

Four short answers.

Why are LEXI's co-signers licensed lawyers only?

LEXI's co-signers are licensed lawyers because a legal opinion is a professional act that bar and registration rules reserve to lawyers, and because the one VARA rule that names a signer (Annex 2 of the Issuance Rulebook, asset-referenced virtual assets) names a duly registered practising lawyer and excludes internal counsel. Compliance officers do not stake or co-sign on LEXI. They appear as readers: the people at exchanges and VASPs who check a credential before relying on it.

Does LEXI take a share of a co-signing lawyer's fee?

No. The protocol retains your firm directly and pays the retainer outside LEXI. LEXI takes no share of your fee, and 0% of protocol revenue goes to the co-signing lawyer. The economics of your practice do not route through a token.

Can a relier slash a co-signing lawyer's stake?

No. Reliers flag disputes; a committee adjudicates them. Slashing requires proven misconduct and is capped in basis points. Nobody is slashed because a regulator disagrees with an opinion, and $LEXI holders never vote on whether a protocol is compliant.

Can a licensed lawyer apply before mainnet?

Yes. LEXI is onboarding a small founding panel of licensed lawyers before mainnet, VARA (Dubai) first. V0 runs on Base Sepolia (testnet) and mainnet is targeted post-audit, Q4 2026. Applying commits you to nothing. No $LEXI token has been issued, so no stake is posted today.
