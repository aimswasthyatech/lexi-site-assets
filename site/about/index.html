About LEXI Protocol

LEXI Protocol is compliance infrastructure for crypto on the India, UAE and US corridor. It turns a licensed lawyer's co-signed legal opinion into a dated, scope-bound credential on Base that the parties it names can verify in one call. VARA first, running on Base Sepolia testnet today; the EU (MiCA) and India (FIU-IND) are V1, next up. Mainnet is targeted post-audit, Q4 2026.

This page is written so that anyone checking LEXI can do it from the page itself: what is built and where it runs, who builds it, how it is designed, what it costs, and what it is not. Every fact is dated.

What LEXI does

What LEXI does, labelled by build state.

Credential issuance runs end to end on testnet today and briefings are published. The rest is in design, in onboarding or on the roadmap, and each item says which.

[On testnet]

Credential issuance for protocols

A protocol submits its documentation once for its VARA question. The LEXI engine, in development on a proprietary corpus of real regulatory work, prepares the first pass as work product. One assigned licensed lawyer runs their own diligence and co-signs a scoped legal opinion, which becomes a dated, scope-bound, non-transferable credential on Base, one per cleared jurisdiction, naming who may rely on it.

See how it works

[V0 design]

Reliance access for named reliers

In the V0 design, a relier named on a credential checks it against Base in one call: status is Live, the scope and opinion hashes match the documents it holds, and its entity is on the reliance list and has accepted the reliance terms. Reading public metadata is free; the gated opinion body carries a fixed USDC reliance fee. The check runs against mainnet records, post-audit.

How a credential is read

[Onboarding]

Co-signing for licensed lawyers

Licensed lawyers co-sign in their own name after their own diligence. The founding panel is VARA first and applications are open. The protocol retains the lawyer's firm directly and pays the retainer outside LEXI, so the client relationship stays where it is. Lawyers stake a USD-pegged ~$5,000 in $LEXI to be eligible; slashing is committee-adjudicated for proven misconduct and capped in basis points, and reliers flag disputes rather than slash.

[V1 · in development]

The LEXI Agent and new jurisdictions

V1 is a fully refreshed LEXI Agent, the engine behind the first pass, rebuilt around long-horizon diligence with persistent agent memory and iterative agentic search across many rulebooks in multiple jurisdictions. It adds new jurisdictions, starting with the EU (MiCA) and India (FIU-IND), so one opinion can span several of them.

[V2 · planned]

Agent connectors and the LEXI Verification Service API

Agent connectors would let the LEXI Agent cross-verify facts a protocol asserts against external tools such as DocuSign and GitHub. The LEXI Verification Service is planned as a standalone API for enterprise compliance and KYB workflows, so an enterprise can check that a counterparty meets a minimum compliance threshold before serving it. US coverage is on the roadmap.

Maturity labels describe build state, never traction. LEXI is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. The engine's output is work product, never the opinion, and no compliance score is published.

Comparisons

What a team compares LEXI with, and what each one attests.

A team that needs a compliance answer on the corridor usually weighs these. Each does a real job, and several sit alongside a LEXI credential. The table describes categories, not vendors; the one named product is Coinbase Verifications, quoted from its own repository.

Fig. 03 · Five things a team compares with a LEXI credential · sources checked at source on 2026-10-16
What a team comparesWhat it attests, and who signsWho may rely on itWhat a LEXI credential does instead
A token legal opinion as a signed letter A licensed lawyer's conclusion on a stated scope, signed and dated on the firm's letterhead. VARA's Guidance on the Virtual Asset Issuance Rulebook (9 April 2026) says a legal opinion given to VARA on an asset-referenced virtual asset issuance must be prepared and signed by a duly registered practising lawyer, not the issuer's in-house counsel, and must be dated, signed and issued on the official letterhead of the practising law firm or sole practitioner. The addressee, under the letter's own reliance language. A counterparty holding a copy cannot check the version, the date or the reliance terms without asking the firm. Keeps the signed opinion and adds the credential: hash commitments to the exact document set, an as-of date, an expiry, a status and a reliance list on Base. It does not replace the letterhead opinion a regulator asks for.
Coinbase Verifications, on Base Attestations by Coinbase about a wallet holder: a verified account, a verified country, a Coinbase One membership. Their repository says the attestations should not be relied upon by you or any third party for any legal, compliance, or contractual purpose. No one, for legal, compliance or contractual purposes, by Coinbase's own terms. Records what a licensed lawyer concluded about a protocol's activity, names the parties that may rely on it, and expires. It carries no identity data about a protocol's end users.
Ethereum Attestation Service (EAS) Open infrastructure on Base for signed, schema-defined attestations by any entity about anything. The EAS documentation says an attestation's credibility hinges on the reputation of the entity making it, and makes no legal-reliance claim. Anyone can read an attestation. Whether to rely on it is the reader's judgement of the attester. One record type with a named licensed lawyer as co-signatory, a USD-pegged stake, committee-adjudicated slashing, an expiry and a reliance list. Non-transferable; the standard is undecided.
Transaction-monitoring and blockchain-analytics vendors Risk scores and alerts on wallets and transactions, produced by software and updated continuously. Vendor pages describe risk exposure, fraud prevention and investigations. The subscribing institution, for its own monitoring. A score is not a legal conclusion. Publishes no risk score and no automated verdict. A protocol with a LEXI credential will usually still need transaction monitoring; the two answer different questions.
On-chain identity and KYC attestations Reusable proofs that a wallet holder completed an identity check with a provider, issued under the provider's terms. Applications that accept that provider's attestation. Not an identity check. The credential concerns a protocol's activity, not its users; the opinion body stays off-chain, and only hash commitments go to Base.
