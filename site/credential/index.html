The credential

The LEXI credential: verify a legal opinion on-chain

Definition

An on-chain legal opinion, defined.

Definition

An on-chain legal opinion is a legal opinion whose signer, date, scope and current status are recorded on a public chain, so the parties it names can check them without asking the firm. A LEXI credential is a co-signed legal opinion committed on-chain. It records who signed, for which jurisdiction, as of which date, until when, under which governing law and for whom, and it binds the opinion's scope and text with two hashes. It is not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator.

The opinion itself never touches the chain. A licensed lawyer reviews the LEXI engine's work product, runs their own diligence and co-signs the opinion. The opinion body stays off-chain, encrypted; the chain holds hash commitments to it and the metadata a relier needs. Reading that metadata is free. V0 records credentials on Base Sepolia, Base's public testnet. Nothing is live on mainnet, and mainnet is targeted post-audit, Q4 2026.

Clearing a jurisdiction means obtaining a co-signed legal opinion covering it, and one credential records one cleared jurisdiction. V0 clears VARA (Dubai); the EU (MiCA) and India (FIU-IND) are V1, next up. The flow that produces a credential is on how LEXI works; what a crypto legal opinion is, and why counterparties ask for one, is on crypto legal opinions.

What lives where

What goes on-chain, and what stays off it.

The relier check

Read a credential in three steps.

Verification is a read against the credential record, in one call. Each step reads one on-chain field.

  1. Status is Live, not Expired or Revoked.

    As-of date, expiry and revocation are on-chain.Does it still speak as of a date I accept?

  2. The scope and opinion hashes match the documents you hold.

    Hash the PDF and the scope text and compare them with the on-chain commitments.Is this the document the lawyer signed?

  3. Your entity is a named relier and has accepted the reliance terms.

    The credential names who may rely on it; the reliance record shows you accepted the terms.Am I expressly authorised to rely?

Live means unexpired and unrevoked. It does not mean the opinion is a current legal conclusion; the as-of date and the regulator events after it tell you that. The corridor tracker lists those events and what each does to an opinion's scope or date.

Reliance

Reliance is explicit, and recorded.

A named relier is a party the credential lists as entitled to rely on the opinion. Reliance attaches only after that party accepts the reliance terms, and each acceptance is written on-chain as a reliance record: a transaction signed by the relier, carrying the fixed USDC fee, that proves reliance was granted on a given date. Under Selective mode, a party that is not named may read the public metadata but cannot rely on the opinion and cannot open its body.

Reliance mode is the on-chain field that says who may rely. Selective, the default, means only the reliers named on the credential. Open means any party may become a relier by accepting the reliance terms and paying the USDC reliance fee. In both modes, reliance attaches only after the terms are accepted and the acceptance is recorded on-chain; reliance is always explicit.

Reliance letters, and what replaces them

A PDF opinion may be relied on only by its addressee and by anyone the firm expressly authorises, and it speaks as of its date. Opinion practice has held both rules for years; the American Bar Association's Core Opinion Principles of 2017 state them in sections 4.1 and 4.2. A party outside the addressee line asks the firm for a reliance letter, which is often granted only as of the original date and sometimes marked as delivered but not to be relied upon. A LEXI credential puts the reliance list, the as-of date and the expiry on-chain, so the question of who may rely, and as of when, has a recorded answer instead of a letter.

Nothing is edited after signing. A refreshed opinion gets a new credential with its own date; the old one is left to expire or is revoked. That is why the second step of the check works: if the document set changes, the scope hash stops matching.

Checking the signatory

The signatory and the firm ID are public on-chain fields, so a relier can check the licence with the issuing bar or register. For a Dubai-registered signatory, the Government of Dubai Legal Affairs Department publishes a public directory of advocates and legal consultants (opens in a new tab) showing status, registration number and licence expiry. Registration-number lookups work; the directory covers Dubai (onshore) registrations only. The specimen on this page shows "A. Signatory", a fictional placeholder.

Limits

What the credential is not.

  • Not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator. It records a licensed lawyer's scoped opinion, nothing more. LEXI is not licensed or endorsed by VARA, and no regulator takes part in recording a credential.
  • Not transferable. A credential is bound to its engagement. The token standard is an open engineering decision, so no standard is named here.
  • Not a compliance score. No number, no rating, no verdict on-chain beyond the opinion itself and its status.
  • Not the opinion body. The reasoning stays off-chain, encrypted, readable by named reliers who have accepted the reliance terms.
  • Not legal advice from LEXI. LEXI is not a law firm. The opinion is the licensed lawyer's, issued to named reliers under a defined scope, and no attorney-client relationship arises from reading a credential.

Questions

Questions reliers ask.

How does a relier verify a LEXI credential?

A named relier runs three checks against the credential record on Base: the status is Live, not Expired or Revoked; the scope and opinion hashes match the documents the relier holds; and the relier's entity is a named relier that has accepted the reliance terms. Reading the public on-chain metadata is free. V0 runs on Base Sepolia testnet, and nothing is live on mainnet.

Is a LEXI credential a licence or a regulator approval?

No. A LEXI credential is a co-signed legal opinion committed on-chain. It is not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator, and LEXI is not licensed or endorsed by VARA. LEXI is not a law firm; the opinion belongs to the licensed lawyer who co-signed it.

Is the credential the legal opinion itself?

No. The credential is metadata plus two hash commitments to a versioned opinion held off-chain. The opinion body stays encrypted in an EU-hosted confidential store and opens only to named reliers who have accepted the reliance terms. The scope text, the assumptions and reservations, and the permitted-use terms are public off-chain.

What does it cost to read a LEXI credential?

Reading the public on-chain metadata is free. Reliance access, meaning the gated opinion body plus an on-chain reliance record, is a fixed USDC fee. Price points publish at mainnet. Holding $LEXI is never required.
