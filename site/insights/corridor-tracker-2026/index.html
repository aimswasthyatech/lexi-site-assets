The tracker follows the regulators wherever they move. LEXI's own coverage is narrower: VARA (Dubai) on Base Sepolia testnet today; the EU (MiCA) and India (FIU-IND) are V1, next up; US coverage is on the roadmap. Nothing is on mainnet. LEXI is not a law firm, and nothing here is legal advice.

What this tracker is

Definition

The corridor tracker lists dated regulator events on the India, UAE, US and EU corridor and states, for each, what it does to a legal opinion's scope or date. Edition 1 covers 30 December 2024 to 16 October 2026 and was re-read at every source on 11 October 2026 and verified again on 16 October 2026. An opinion speaks as of its date; this tracker tells a relier which rules changed after that date. 7 earlier standing instruments that the window's rows depend on are included and marked baseline.

What "effect on an opinion" means

A legal opinion answers a defined question under named law, on stated facts, as of its date. Two things can go wrong with it later: the law it read can change (a scope effect) or the facts it relied on can move (a date effect). The effect column labels each row with one or both. It says what a relier reading an opinion dated before the event should check, in verification terms only. It does not say whether any project is licensable, and it describes no licensing or registration steps. That is a question for counsel, not a conclusion LEXI gives.

The snapshot rule

Some rows are counts rather than events: a register size, a list of orders, an absence of orders. Each is a snapshot with its own as-of date, printed in the date cell, and none is presented as stable. A negative row (no order listed, no text published) is stated with the date it was checked and re-checked in the next edition. Where a regulator has not published an instrument's text, the date cell reads "not published at source" rather than a guess, on the page and in the CSV.

Where the rows come from

Every row names the regulator, statute, Official Journal, Gazette or parliamentary document it was read from, with the URL. No law-firm summary is cited in any row. India rows come only from FIU-IND, the Gazette, SEBI and parliamentary answers; US rows only from official sources. Two FIU-IND documents are scanned images; their rows were OCR-verified against the scans and are marked so in the CSV.

How to read a row against a credential

As-of date against event date. A LEXI credential records the opinion's as-of date on-chain. Take that date, go to the opinion's jurisdiction above, and read every row dated after it. A row marked scope means the law the opinion read has moved and the opinion cannot be read to cover the change. A row marked date means a fact the opinion relied on is now dated and should be checked again. An opinion does not update itself after its date; that is why a credential carries an expiry and a status (Live, Expired or Revoked) instead of leaving the date in a PDF footer.

Register snapshot against credential status. A register row (59 licensed VASPs on VARA's register, 370 CASP rows on ESMA's, 54 registered VDA service providers in a parliamentary answer) is a count on its own date. A credential's status is a different fact about a different thing. Check the register as of the date you rely, not as of this tracker's snapshot, and check the credential's status on-chain at the same time.

Not published at source. Where the date cell says so, an opinion that cites the instrument should state which version it read and when, and a relier should treat that statement as part of the opinion's scope.

The relier check. The three steps a relier runs on any credential (status is Live; the scope and opinion hashes match the documents you hold; your entity is a named relier that accepted the reliance terms) are on the credential page, and in a listing team's language on the page for exchanges and reliers. The terms this tracker uses (as-of date, named relier, reliance mode, scope hash, status) are defined in the glossary. Reading public on-chain metadata is free; the relier check runs against mainnet records after the audit, and nothing is on mainnet today.

A LEXI credential is a co-signed legal opinion committed on-chain. It is not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator. LEXI is not licensed or endorsed by VARA.
