Why verifiable legal opinions, now

In 2025 and 2026, regulators in Dubai, the EU and the US wrote into their own rules and templates what opinion practice has always assumed: an opinion has a signer, a date, a set of assumptions and a shelf life. This briefing sets out what changed, what a PDF cannot carry, and what a credential on Base records instead. V0 runs on Base Sepolia (testnet); nothing is live on mainnet.

By Lex Arbitrage (opens in a new tab), Co-Founder and CEO · Published 2026-10-11 · Last updated 2026-10-16

LEXI is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. A credential is not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator.

In one passage

A legal opinion speaks as of its date and may be relied on only by the people it names. Regulators in Dubai, the EU and the US now say so in their own rules and templates: since 9 April 2026 Dubai's VARA expects an opinion on an asset-referenced issuance to be signed by a duly registered practising lawyer, dated, on letterhead and revised when facts change; the EU's supervisors have applied a field-by-field template for asset-referenced token opinions since 12 May 2025, and reported zero such tokens authorised by 1 September 2026; the SEC replaced its 2019 framework on 17 March 2026. A verifiable legal opinion is one whose signer, date, scope and current status can be checked by the parties it names without asking the firm. That is what a LEXI credential records on Base. V0 runs on Base Sepolia testnet.

The claim

Every serious opinion letter already carries four facts: who signed it, when, on what documents and assumptions, and who may rely on it. Opinion practice treats those facts as the whole value of the document: a closing opinion "speaks as of its date" and may be relied on only by its addressee and anyone "expressly authorized to rely"13.

What changed in 2025 and 2026 is that regulators in Dubai, the EU and the US wrote the same four facts into rules and templates. An opinion that cannot show its signer, its date, its scope and its current status is now the odd document in the file. This is general information, not legal advice, and it speaks as of 16 October 2026. The corridor regulation tracker lists each dated event with its source.

Regulators now say who may sign, and in what form

VARA's Guidance on the Virtual Asset Issuance Rulebook (9 April 2026) says a legal opinion given to VARA on an asset-referenced virtual asset issuance must be prepared and signed by a duly registered practising lawyer, not the issuer's in-house counsel, and must be dated, signed and issued on the firm's letterhead1. The guidance runs to six headed sections on the opinion: authorship and qualification, independence and objectivity, scope, assumptions, reliance and limitations, form and submission, and ongoing validity and amendments. On assumptions it is specific: the opinion "must clearly articulate any assumptions, limitations, and sources of information on which the lawyer has relied", and must say which facts came from the issuer1.

The legal opinion must be prepared and signed by a duly registered practising lawyer.VARA, Guidance on the Virtual Asset Issuance Rulebook, 9 April 2026, item 32

The scope of that rule is narrow, and the narrowness matters. The guidance is indicative by its own introduction, not a substitute for independent legal advice, and it glosses two rules in Annex 2 of the Issuance Rulebook (19 May 2025), which covers asset-referenced virtual assets: Rule III.B.2 says a legal opinion may be required, and Rule III.C.4 requires one from issuers holding reserve assets2. No other rule in the Issuance Rulebook, the Broker-Dealer Services Rulebook or the 2023 Regulations asks for a legal opinion, and the package a Licensed Distributor files for a Category 2 issuance carries none3. On LEXI, co-signers are licensed lawyers by design; that is LEXI's choice, and LEXI is not licensed or endorsed by VARA.

The UAE also gained a federal layer on 1 January 2026, when the SCA became the Capital Market Authority under Federal Decree-Laws 32 and 33 of 2025, with virtual assets inside the federal perimeter; the one-year status-adjustment period under Article 83 of Decree-Law 33 runs to 1 January 2027 and may be extended4. For an opinion that is a scope question: which regulator's rules it covers, and as of which date. Our briefing on the UAE CMA has the detail, and the VARA compliance page covers licensing by activity in Dubai.

Regulators now expect opinions to stay current

The same VARA guidance makes the issuer responsible for keeping an Annex 2 opinion current throughout the review, and says that where there are material changes in the structure, rights or regulatory treatment of the asset, "the lawyer is obliged to issue a revised or supplemental opinion to VARA"1. That is a regulator writing an expiry condition into an opinion.

The stronger rule for reliers needs no asset-referenced caveat. Part IV of VARA's Broker-Dealer Services Rulebook requires a Licensed Distributor to ensure that its due diligence on an issuer and its asset "remains up-to-date and accurate at all times" while it distributes (Rule IV.E.1), to suspend distribution the moment it knows or suspects the asset "no longer complies" (Rule IV.E.2), and on any material change to redo the diligence and resubmit it to VARA, covering every change since the last submission (Rule IV.E.3)3. Neither rule mentions a legal opinion, a credential or LEXI. A distributor under those rules needs every document in its file to carry a date and a way to tell whether it still holds. A co-signed opinion with an as-of date, an expiry and a status is evidence that distributor can file and re-check, not something VARA asks for.

The EU wrote a template, and launches still wait on opinions

On 10 December 2024 the three European supervisory authorities published Joint Guidelines JC 2024 28 under Article 97 of MiCA, applying from 12 May 2025. They carry three instruments: a template for the explanation an offeror of an ordinary crypto-asset attaches to its white paper (Annex A), a template for the legal opinion that accompanies an asset-referenced token application (Annex B), and a standardised classification test (Annex C)5. Tokens under Title II need an explanation, not an opinion. The legal-opinion template exists for asset-referenced tokens.

Annex B is a field list. An EU asset-referenced token opinion states the date on which it is issued; who issues it, with evidence of the ability to practise; a declaration of conflicts of interest; the dated final white paper, attached; the Member States concerned; and the applicable law5. It has no expiry field and no field for who may rely on it. The ESA template may be completed by an in-house or external legal adviser who can evidence the ability to practise. LEXI goes further: on LEXI the co-signer is a licensed lawyer.

The supervisors' own 2026 reports describe what the template did and did not solve. The EBA's response to the Commission's MiCA review (24 September 2026) reports 39 electronic money tokens issued under MiCA and zero asset-referenced tokens authorised as at 1 September 2026, and says the launch of tokenised investment products may be delayed "due to protracted processes involving legal opinions and supervisory dialogues"6. ESMA's response (30 September 2026) records that "1 July 2026 marked the end of the MiCA transitional period", says "classification remains one of the most important issues under MiCA", and proposes binding ESMA classification opinions7. A relier reading an EU opinion now has two facts to pin: the date of the opinion and the register status of its counterparty on that date. On LEXI, the EU (MiCA) is V1, next up.

The US re-set the baseline on 17 March 2026

On 17 March 2026 the SEC issued an interpretation (Release No. 33-11412; 34-105020, effective 23 March 2026) that sorts crypto assets into five categories by their characteristics, uses and functions: digital commodities, digital collectibles, digital tools, stablecoins and digital securities. It says the first three "are not themselves securities", adds that "a non-security crypto asset can be offered and sold subject to an investment contract, which is a security", and supersedes the SEC staff's 2019 framework for investment-contract analysis8. Those are the SEC's words; the second sentence is the one an opinion has to work through.

The effect on an opinion is the date. A US securities opinion written before 17 March 2026 rests on a framework that no longer applies, and a relier reading one today needs its as-of date first. The baseline is still moving: Regulation Crypto Assets, published in the Federal Register on 21 August 2026, proposes two exemptions from Securities Act registration and a conditional carve-out from the term "investment contract", with comments due on or before 20 October 2026. It is a proposal, not a rule in force9. US coverage on LEXI is on the roadmap.

A compliance officer at an exchange or a Licensed Distributor can cite every item above by title and date. The opinion in the same file should be citable the same way: by signer, date, scope and status.

What a PDF cannot carry

Opinion practice limits an opinion to its date and its addressees13. A PDF carries both limits as words and offers no way to check either. The exchange that receives it cannot verify the signature on its own, cannot tell whether the opinion was later withdrawn, cannot tell whether the version in the data room is the version the lawyer signed, and cannot tell whether its own entity is authorised to rely. Some listing teams require an opinion in writing; most run an internal legal review and keep a file. Our briefing on how exchanges verify a token legal opinion records what each listing team publishes.

Some opinion sellers advertise acceptance rates with exchanges. Nobody outside the seller can check them, because the opinion is a PDF with no public record. A credential's status, scope and reliance list are checkable by anyone, free.

What a credential carries

A LEXI credential takes the four facts the rules above keep asking for and records them where the parties the opinion names can read them. The licensed lawyer still writes and signs the opinion after their own diligence; the LEXI engine prepares a first pass as work product, never the opinion, and no AI signs anything. The co-signed opinion is then committed on Base as a credential: the signatory and firm ID, the jurisdiction and governing law, the as-of date and expiry, a status of Live, Expired or Revoked, the reliance mode, and a scope hash and an opinion hash that bind the exact scope text and opinion document. The scope text, assumptions and reservations and permitted-use terms sit off-chain in public. The opinion body stays off-chain, encrypted, open to the named reliers against an on-chain reliance record. A new opinion means a new credential, never an edit to the old one.

The two sets of facts rhyme: the signer, the date, the stated assumptions and the duty to revise on the VARA side; the issue date, the evidence of ability to practise and the attached white paper on the EU side; the date and the authorised reliers in opinion practice. A credential adds what none of them carries: an expiry, a current status and a named-relier list readable by anyone. The relier's check is three steps.

  1. Status is Live, not Expired or Revoked.

    As-of date, expiry and revocation are on-chain.Does it still speak as of a date I accept?

  2. The scope and opinion hashes match the documents you hold.

    Hash the PDF and the scope text and compare them with the on-chain commitments.Is this the document the lawyer signed?

  3. Your entity is a named relier and has accepted the reliance terms.

    The credential names who may rely on it; the reliance record shows you accepted the terms.Am I expressly authorised to rely?

Reading the public on-chain metadata is free. Reliance access, which opens the gated body and writes the reliance record, is a fixed USDC fee; price points publish at mainnet. The page for exchanges, VASPs and Licensed Distributors puts the check in a compliance file's terms.

What it is not

A credential is not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator. It is not a filing with VARA, the CMA, an EU authority, the SEC or FIU-IND, and it does not satisfy Annex 2 or any other rule on its own; the lawyer's signed opinion does whatever work a regulator asks of it, in the form that regulator prescribes. LEXI is not licensed or endorsed by VARA. A credential is not an identity or KYC attestation and carries no data about a protocol's end users. No risk score is written on-chain. LEXI is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

Questions

What makes a legal opinion verifiable?

A legal opinion is verifiable when the parties it names can check its signer, its date, the exact documents it covers and its current status without asking the firm that wrote it. A LEXI credential records those facts on Base: the signatory and firm ID, the as-of date and expiry, the scope and opinion hashes, a status of Live, Expired or Revoked, and the named reliers. V0 runs on Base Sepolia (testnet); nothing is live on mainnet.

Does VARA require a lawyer-signed opinion for every token?

No. The VARA rules that name a signer sit in Annex 2 of the Virtual Asset Issuance Rulebook and concern asset-referenced virtual assets: Rule III.B.2 says an opinion may be required, and Rule III.C.4 requires one from issuers holding reserve assets. VARA's Guidance of 9 April 2026 says such an opinion must be prepared and signed by a duly registered practising lawyer, not the issuer's in-house counsel. The package a Licensed Distributor files for a Category 2 issuance carries no legal-opinion requirement. LEXI's licensed-lawyer rule is LEXI's own design choice, and LEXI is not licensed or endorsed by VARA.

Is a LEXI credential a regulator approval?

No. A LEXI credential is a co-signed legal opinion committed on-chain. It is not a licence, a registration, or an approval by any regulator, and it is not a filing with VARA, the CMA, an EU authority, the SEC or FIU-IND. LEXI is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.
