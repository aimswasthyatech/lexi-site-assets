The UAE's federal CMA in 2026: Decree-Laws 32 and 33, the April framework and Resolution 16

On 1 January 2026 the Securities and Commodities Authority became the Capital Market Authority, and virtual assets moved inside a federal perimeter that now sits beside VARA. This refresh adds the CMA's 2026 instruments and states, for each one, what it does to a legal opinion's scope and date.

By Lex Arbitrage (opens in a new tab), Co-Founder and CEO · Published 2026-07-03 · Last updated 2026-10-16

Last reviewed at source: 16 October 2026, at uaecma.gov.ae, legislation.uaecma.gov.ae and vara.ae. Where a text is not published, this briefing says so.

In one passage

On 1 January 2026 the UAE's Securities and Commodities Authority became the Capital Market Authority (CMA) under Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2025, and Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2025 brought virtual assets inside the federal capital-market perimeter. The CMA's regulation of virtual-asset service providers, Resolution No. (04/Chairman) of 2026, was issued on 13 February 2026 and has been in force since 28 February 2026. A legal opinion on UAE-federal activity dated on or after 1 January 2026 names the CMA, cites Decree-Law 33's licensable activities (including 'Promotion' and 'Activities and services related to Virtual Assets') and its Art. 39 listing-and-registration rule, and states which CMA instruments it read and when. Checked at uaecma.gov.ae and legislation.uaecma.gov.ae on 16 October 2026.

October 2026 update

The July edition of this briefing covered the two decree-laws. Since then the CMA has issued its virtual-asset instrument, announced a framework and widened the perimeter once. Each item below carries its effective date where a primary source states one, and what it does to an opinion.

Decree-Laws 32 and 33 of 2025
In force 1 January 202613. The SCA became the CMA; virtual assets became a Financial Product and "Activities and services related to Virtual Assets" a licensable Financial Activity. Scope: a UAE-federal opinion dated on or after 1 January 2026 names the CMA. Date: anything analysed under the 2023 regime needs a re-read.
Resolution No. (04/Chairman) of 2026
The CMA's regulation of virtual-asset service providers and the alternative trading system operator, also cited as 4/R.M. Issued 13 February 2026, published in the Official Gazette on 27 February 2026 and in force from 28 February 2026, as the CMA Rulebook records12. It approves three modules and repeals Decision No. (26/Chairman) of 2023 (Arts. 1 and 6), with one year from the effective date to meet the module requirements (Art. 3). Scope: the instrument a UAE-federal VASP opinion reads. Date: 28 February 2026.
The Virtual Assets Framework, 13 April 2026
Announced by press release: five modules, eight regulated activities, no decision number and no effective date7. The eight activities carry the same names as the eight in Art. 12 of the General Module approved by Resolution 04/202612. Scope: the activity list an opinion maps a project against. Date: 13 April 2026 is a publication date; the regulation itself has been in force since 28 February 2026.
Resolution No. (16/Chairman) of 2026
Issued 25 June 2026, in force on issuance4. Entities licensed by the Central Bank, other than insurers, may practise the Resolution 04/2026 activities. Scope: the set of possible UAE reliers widens toward banks and payment firms. Date: 25 June 2026.
Cross-border reach and finfluencers
Decree-Law 33 Art. 2(1)(d), in force 1 January 2026, reaches any person targeting clients within the State even from outside it, and Art. 3(1)(i) lists "Promotion" as a licensable activity3. Finfluencers sit under Resolution No. (10/R.M) of 2025; the CMA's authorised-finfluencer list page is stamped last updated 04/05/2026 (5 April 2026, read month-first as the site's other English dates are) and its table was empty when read8. Scope: whether a given promotion into the UAE sits inside the perimeter is a scope question for counsel, not a conclusion LEXI gives.

What changed on 1 January 2026

Two federal decree-laws, issued in 2025, entered into force on 1 January 20262.

  • Federal Decree-Law No. (32) of 2025 on the Capital Market Authority makes the Capital Market Authority the legal successor of the Securities and Commodities Authority and replaces the old designation wherever it appears in legislation (Art. 2(2) and 2(3)). Federal Law No. 4 of 2000, which created the SCA, is repealed (Art. 29). In force 1 January 2026 (Art. 30)1. The English copy leaves its issue date blank, so this briefing gives none.
  • Federal Decree-Law No. (33) of 2025 on the Regulation of the Capital Market, issued 1 October 2025, rewrites the scope of regulated financial activity and puts virtual assets inside it. In force 1 January 2026 (Art. 85)3.

sca.gov.ae now redirects to uaecma.gov.ae, so every citation in this briefing uses the new domain.

What Decree-Law 33 puts inside the federal perimeter

Art. 1 of Decree-Law 33 defines the asset the federal law now reaches, and the definition decides which regulator an opinion must name3:

Decree-Law 33 of 2025, Art. 1

"Virtual Assets: A digital representation of value that can be traded or transferred digitally and may be used for investment purposes, excluding digital representations of fiat currencies, Securities, or other funds, without prejudice to the Central Bank's jurisdiction over monetary instruments or instruments related to payment methods or value preservation."

The same article lists "Virtual Assets for investment purposes" among Financial Products. Four more articles build the perimeter; each is a scoping fact for an opinion, not a conclusion about any one project:

  • Art. 3(1) lists the Financial Activities that need a CMA licence or approval. Item (i) is "Promotion" and item (y) is "Activities and services related to Virtual Assets". Art. 3(2) says no person may carry out any of them within the State without a CMA licence or approval.
  • Art. 2(1)(d) applies the law to any person targeting clients within the State, even if the activity is conducted outside the State or from a Financial Free Zone. This is the statutory basis for cross-border exposure.
  • Art. 2(4) carves out activities licensed by the Central Bank and the Financial Free Zones, where the DIFC and ADGM sit.
  • Art. 39 gives the CMA the regulation of virtual-asset trading and the procedures for registering virtual assets. Trading in a virtual asset within the State is prohibited unless the asset is admitted to the official list of a CMA-licensed platform and registered with the CMA. That rule sits in the decree-law itself.

Art. 83 gives everyone subject to the law one year from entry into force to adjust their status, and lets the Board extend that period. No instrument names a calendar deadline, so this briefing does not either. Whether a particular site, offer or campaign falls inside Art. 2(1)(d) and Art. 3(1)(i) is a scope question for counsel, not a conclusion LEXI gives. An opinion says which articles it read, as of which date, and for which activity.

Resolution 04/2026: the VASP regulation, in force since 28 February 2026

The CMA's virtual-asset instrument is the Chairman of the Board's Resolution No. (04/Chairman) of 2026 Concerning the Regulation of Virtual Assets Service Providers and the Alternative Trading System Operator, also cited as 4/R.M. Its text is published on the CMA Rulebook at legislation.uaecma.gov.ae, which records it as issued on 13 February 2026, published in the Official Gazette on 27 February 2026, effective 28 February 2026 and in force12. The Board approved it at its meeting of 26 December 2025. Art. 1 approves the attached Regulation in three modules: a General Framework Module, a Business Regulation Module and an Alternative Trading System Module. Art. 3 gives licensed entities and accredited persons one year from the effective date to meet the Business Regulation and Alternative Trading System modules, and Art. 6 repeals Decision No. (26/Chairman) of 2023 on the virtual-asset platform operator and the VASP provisions of the 2021 Rulebook, keeping their licensing and accreditation conditions until the regularisation period ends. Art. 7 brings the resolution into force on the day after gazette publication. Art. 12 of the General Module deems the VASP activities financial activities and regulates eight of them: dealing as principal, dealing as agent, providing custody, arranging custody, operating a multilateral trading facility, providing investment advice, portfolio management and arranging investment deals. Resolution 16/2026 cites the same title in its recitals, in English and, as 04/ر.م, in Arabic45.

The resolution does not appear on the CMA's Latest Regulations page, which listed only Decisions 11 and 13 and Resolutions 16 and 18 of 2026 when read on 16 October 20266; it sits in the Rulebook instead. The 11 October edition of this briefing said the text, issue date and effective date were not published at source. That was wrong: they were on the Rulebook, and this edition corrects it. The July edition's phrase "supersedes the 2023 framework" is now a sourced fact in the narrower form Art. 6 gives it: the 2023 platform-operator decision is repealed, with its licensing conditions kept through the regularisation period.

What the CMA announced on 13 April 2026

On Monday 13 April 2026 the CMA published a press release titled "The CMA Issues the Virtual Assets Framework, Establishing an Integrated Regulatory Regime Comprising Five Core Modules and Eight Regulated Activities"7. The five modules are General Requirements, Conduct of Business, Alternative Trading System, Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing, and Prudential Requirements. The eight regulated activities, expanded from three, are Dealing in Virtual Assets as Principal, Dealing in Virtual Assets as Agent, Providing Custody, Arranging Custody, Arranging Investment Deals, Providing Investment Advice, Portfolio Management, and Operating a Multilateral Trading Facility. The release says the framework is aligned with IOSCO and FATF and states its principle in seven words7.

"same activity, same risk, same regulatory outcome"UAE Capital Market Authority, press release, 13 April 2026

The release names no decision number, no effective date and no transition period, and does not mention VARA, the DIFC or ADGM. It does not say which instrument it describes. The eight activities it names are, name for name, the eight in Art. 12 of the General Module that Resolution 04/2026 approved12; the release's five modules do not match the three that the resolution's Art. 1 lists, and this briefing does not reconcile the two. For an opinion, the eight activities are the list a project's activities get mapped against. Art. 12 has carried them since 28 February 2026, and the release restated them on 13 April 2026.

What Resolution 16/2026 does

Resolution No. (16/Chairman) of 2026 Concerning the Permission for Entities Licensed by the Central Bank to Practice Activities Licensed by the Authority was approved by the Board on 9 June 2026, issued in Abu Dhabi on 25 June 2026 and entered into force on the date of its issuance (Art. 2)4. It does one thing: Art. 1 permits entities licensed by the Central Bank, other than insurance companies, to practise any of the activities under Resolution 04/2026.

It widens the perimeter and says nothing about promotion, finfluencers or cross-border activity. For a relier it matters because the UAE counterparties able to hold a CMA-regulated virtual-asset activity now include banks and payment firms, and an opinion addressed to such a relier names the licence under which it acts. The other 2026 instruments on the CMA's regulations page, Decisions 11 (30 April 2026) and 13 (2 June 2026), amend capital-adequacy and broker-financing rules and do not concern virtual assets6.

What stays in force from 2022

Decree-Law 32 Art. 27(3) keeps Cabinet Resolution No. 111 of 2022 on the regulation of virtual assets and Cabinet Resolution No. 112 of 2022 on delegating virtual-asset competencies in force, with the resolutions issued under them, to the extent they do not conflict with the new law, until repealed, amended or replaced. Art. 27(4) continues earlier Cabinet and CMA resolutions on the same terms1.

The CMA's older guidelines on virtual assets and their service providers (document code LD-F-23, V1.0) still sit on its circulars page. They were issued under Cabinet Resolution 111 of 2022 and Decision No. (26/Chairman) of 2023 and describe the 2023 regime9. They are cited here as lineage, never as current law.

Where VARA sits beside the CMA

VARA describes itself as the sole authority regulating virtual assets across Dubai's free zones and mainland, except within the jurisdiction of the Dubai International Financial Centre11. Decree-Law 33 Art. 2(4)(d) excludes the Financial Free Zones from the federal law3, and the two law-firm summaries that discuss the point agree: Clyde & Co says VARA remains the competent authority for virtual-asset businesses established in Dubai outside the DIFC, and Horizlaw says VARA will still be the governing regulator for Dubai-established virtual-asset entities10. Dubai teams therefore read two levels of rulebook: VARA's per-activity rulebooks for a Dubai entity, and the federal activity list under Decree-Law 33 for anything that targets clients in the State. How the CMA's eight activities map onto VARA's licensed activities is a scoping question an opinion addresses; no published text settles it.

On LEXI, VARA (Dubai) is the V0 jurisdiction, running on Base Sepolia testnet today. The VARA compliance page sets out where a co-signed opinion fits in that journey. The EU (MiCA) and India (FIU-IND) are V1, next up. Nothing is on mainnet yet.

What this does to an opinion's scope and date

An opinion speaks as of its date. In the UAE the date became load-bearing on 1 January 2026 and moved again on 28 February, 13 April and 25 June. Four consequences follow for anyone who co-signs or relies on a UAE opinion.

  • The regulator named. A UAE-federal opinion dated on or after 1 January 2026 names the CMA, not the SCA, and states that Decree-Law 33 lists "Activities and services related to Virtual Assets" and "Promotion" as licensable activities.
  • The instruments read. The opinion lists which CMA instruments it read and when, Resolution 04/2026 and its three modules included, with the Rulebook version date it read.
  • The reliers named. After 25 June 2026 a Central Bank-licensed entity can practise the Resolution 04/2026 activities, so a relier list that once stopped at VASPs may now include a bank or a payment firm.
  • The re-read. Anything analysed under the 2023 regime, or before 28 February 2026, is re-checked against the new perimeter before a relier accepts it.

Questions

Is the SCA still the regulator?

No. Under Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2025 the Securities and Commodities Authority became the Capital Market Authority on 1 January 2026, as its legal successor in all rights, obligations and contracts, and the designation is replaced wherever it appears in legislation. sca.gov.ae now redirects to uaecma.gov.ae. A legal opinion on UAE-federal activity dated on or after 1 January 2026 names the CMA.

Does Resolution 16/2026 change who may offer virtual-asset activities?

Yes, at the federal level. Resolution No. (16/Chairman) of 2026, issued 25 June 2026 and in force on issuance, permits entities licensed by the Central Bank, other than insurance companies, to practise the activities under Resolution 04/2026. It does not regulate marketing, finfluencers or cross-border promotion. For a relier, the set of UAE counterparties that may hold a CMA-regulated activity now includes banks and payment firms.

Where is the text of Resolution 04/2026?

On the CMA Rulebook at legislation.uaecma.gov.ae, which records Resolution No. (04/Chairman) of 2026 as issued on 13 February 2026, published in the Official Gazette on 27 February 2026 and in force from 28 February 2026. It is not on the CMA's Latest Regulations page, which listed only Decisions 11 and 13 and Resolutions 16 and 18 of 2026 when read on 16 October 2026. An earlier edition of this briefing said the text was unpublished; that was wrong and is corrected here.
